Why cheap power could matter more than clean power in the push for net zero

Reaching net zero has become a major goal for governments around the world as countries attempt to reduce carbon emissions and slow climate change. Much of the discussion focuses on producing cleaner electricity through renewable energy sources such as wind, solar, and hydroelectric power. However, some experts argue that the cost of electricity may be just as important—if not more important—than how clean it is. Cheap power could play a key role in encouraging people and businesses to adopt greener technologies and reduce carbon emissions faster.

One of the biggest challenges in achieving net zero is encouraging people to switch away from fossil fuels. Electric vehicles, heat pumps, and electric heating systems are all designed to replace petrol cars and gas boilers. While these technologies are cleaner, they often depend on electricity to operate. If electricity prices remain high, households may avoid switching because the running costs feel too expensive. In this case, even clean electricity may not lead to lower emissions if people continue relying on cheaper fossil fuel alternatives.

Affordable electricity could also improve public support for environmental policies. Many people support action on climate change, but rising living costs can make environmental changes seem difficult or unfair. If renewable energy or energy systems result in lower bills, people are more likely to see net zero policies positively. Cheap power may therefore encourage wider acceptance of green technologies and government climate strategies.

"WeBusinesses are another important factor in the transition to net zero. Industries such as manufacturing, transport, and technology increasingly rely on electrification to reduce emissions. Lower electricity prices can help businesses invest in electric machinery, cleaner transport systems, and sustainable production methods without significantly increasing costs. This could help reduce industrial carbon emissions while maintaining economic growth

However, supporters of clean power argue that cleaner electricity is still essential. Renewable energy sources reduce dependence on fossil fuels, improve energy security, and help lower greenhouse gas emissions over time. They suggest that as renewable technology becomes more efficient, clean electricity will naturally become cheaper. In this view, focusing on clean power today could eventually lead to affordable energy in the future.
